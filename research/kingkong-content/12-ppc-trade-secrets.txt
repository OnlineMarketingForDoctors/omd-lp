### What is Google adwords?
Before we jump into how to use Google Adwords or manage an Adwords campaign, let’s talk about what, exactly, Google Adwords (or, as some people call it, Ad Words Google) is.
Google Adwords is a pay-per-click (PPC) ad platform—which means that you put up an ad on Google and then you pay a fee to Google every time a user clicks on your ad.
Now, if you’re wondering “how does adwords work?,” there are two main components to Google adwords: the Google Search Network and Google Display Ads. Each allows you to create a Google ad targeted to your specific audience—but the way Google shows those ads and drives clicks is completely different.
With the Google Search Network, you create a text-based ad that will show up on the SERPs when users search for a specific keyword. So, for example, let’s say you own a graphic design business in Melbourne and you want to create an ad on Google to engage new customers. You would create a text ad for your business and then bid on graphic design-related keywords (like “graphic designer in Melbourne” or “Melbourne logo design”). Then, when people searched those keywords, they’d see your ad—and, if they clicked on it, you’d pay Google for the lead.
The Google Display Network is a completely different ballgame. While Google Search Network shows text-based ads directly to users in the Google platform, the Google Display Network allows you to place banner ads (also known as display ads) on websites you think your ideal customer would visit (so, for example, if you own a construction company that specializes in building new homes in Sydney, you might place banner ads on local real estate websites).
Google Search Network and Google Display Network are both effective—but in different ways and for different reasons. If you can choose good adwords, Google Search Network is an incredible way to get your company in front of customers when they’re in dire need of your products or services. If you want to convert your website visitors, running a retargeting Adwords campaign on the Google Display Network can be a slam dunk. The best strategy is going to depend on what end result you’re going for—and where you’re targeting potential customers in the buying cycle.
### How to set up a Google Adwords campaign
Alright, so now that we covered the basics of what Google Adwords is, let’s talk about how, exactly, to get started with the platform—and set up a Google adwords campaign.
First things first—before you can get started with Google Ads, you need a Google account. If you don’t already have a Google account or Gmail address, you’ll need to create one before you move forward with creating a campaign in Google AdWords.
Once your account is all set up, go to ads.google.com. Think of this Adwords landing page as Ground Zero for your AdWords management and paid advertising on Google; this is where you can focus on creating Google ads, adwords optimization, and analyzing adwords competition using Google Adwords.
Once you’re on the Adwords manager homepage, click “Campaigns” in the left sidebar. In the center of the page, click “New Campaign.” From there, you can choose what kind of campaign you want to move forward with (including search, display, shopping, and video) and whether the end goal for your campaign is sales, leads, or website traffic. Then, you can select how you plan to achieve those goals (so, for example, is success driving traffic to a landing page or booking phone calls?).
From there, you can choose whether you want to focus on Google Search or Google Display Network, the geographic location where you want to focus your Google ads (so, for example, in a specific city, state, or zip code), your ad language, your bidding strategy (so, for example, do you want your ads to be spread equally throughout the day or focused during a specific time period?), your budget, and the start and end dates of your campaign.

### Adwords Quality Score: What It Is And Why It’s Important
Choosing the right keywords is one of the key drivers for success with your Google adwords campaign. But there’s another key factor that you have to get right if you want your Google Ad Word campaigns to succeed—and that’s nailing a high Adwords Quality Score.
Google wants their searchers to have the best ad experience possible—and in order to do that, they want to make sure they’re showing highly relevant ads to whatever users are searching for. So, for example, you can’t bid on a keyword for graphic design and show an ad for a car dealership; that would be irrelevant to the user—a big no-no for Google.
When you create an ad on Google Adwords, Google evaluates it for relevancy and assigns it an Adwords Quality Score. Your Quality Score will determine how relevant you are to your keywords—and can have a huge impact on how much you pay per click on each of your ads.
When you write a Google ad, you need to ask yourself “how can I make my adwords campaigns more relevant to the audience I’m serving?” The more relevant your ads, the higher your Adwords Quality Score—and the more likely your ads will be shown (and shown at a low CPC!) to your target audience.

### Top Google Adwords tips
Ok, so marketing with Google Adwords is clearly a good idea for your business. But what are the top Google Adwords tips for finding success with the platform?
- Get your Adwords Quality score up. We mentioned this before, but it’s so important, we’ll mention it again—if you want to get the most out of your campaigns, you need a high Adwords Quality Score. Make sure your ads are relevant to the keywords you’re bidding on—and continue to optimize them to get the lowest CPC possible.
- Start small. If you’re new to Google Website Adwords, you don’t want to set a $10,000 budget straight out of the gate. Start small by testing out a few ads at a low budget. Optimize them until they’re performing well and then roll out a bigger budget. By keeping your costs low until you figure out how to optimize your ads, you can stretch your budget and spend your money when you know your ads will drive results.
- Find the keyword “sweet spot.” When choosing the keywords for your Google ads, you don’t want to go with something that’s affordable but doesn’t have any search volume. On the flip side, you don’t want to go with a keyword that a ton of people are searching for—but is going to cost you an insane amount of cash for every click. When it comes to keywords, the key to success is finding the “sweet spot”—keywords that have enough search volume to drive real results for your business, but not so much competition that the CPC completely drains your budget.
- Split test your ads. If you want your ads to perform at the highest level, you need to test them—and split testing (also known as A/B testing) is a great way to do it. Create an ad and then change one small detail, like the title or CTA. Then, run both versions of the ad and see which performs better. Once you know which ad connects with your audience, repeat the same process with another small detail—and so on and so forth until your ad is performing like crazy.
