### Facebook advertising 101

### Setting up a business page on Facebook
Before you move forward with advertising on Facebook—and create your first Facebook ad—you’ll want to set up a business page on Facebook. Facebook business page setup lets Facebook know that you’re more than just an individual user looking to connect with old friends from high school—you’re a
Facebook business looking to advertise, run your first Facebook campaign, and drive engagement, opt-ins, and sales through the platform.
Facebook business page setup is simple. Go to Facebook.com/business; in the top right hand corner, you’ll see a drop down menu. Select “Create a Page.” Once there, you can choose the option that best suits your business (either “Business or Brand” or “Community or Public Figure”) and then add your page name and business category. So, if we were making a Facebook business page, the page name would be “King Kong” and the business category would be “marketing services.”
Once you’ve added in your page name and business category, congratulations! You’ve got a business page on Facebook. Once your page is set up, you can customize it with photos, contact information, and anything else you want to add in about your company.
### Getting started with your first Facebook ad
Now that you have a piece of Facebook real estate, you can start to advertising on Face book. Go back to the Facebook.com/business URL and click “Create an Ad” in the top right corner. This will take you to the Facebook ads manager—which is where the real magic happens.
The Facebook ads manager is not only the place where you create your Facebook ad campaign—it’s also the place where you can hone in on your ideal audience.
Ads on Facebook have some of the most powerful targeting capabilities in the advertising and marketing industry. Face book marketing allows you to create custom audiences—and then create extremely relevant ads for those audiences.
Your business Facebook account allows you to narrow down your audience based on location, age, gender, interests—you can even create audiences of people who like or follow your competitors. This ability to niche down your audience is incredibly powerful; by creating custom audiences for each sector of your ideal demographic, you can create incredibly targeted ads and messaging—and the more targeted your ads and messaging, the higher your conversion rate.
If you want your Facebook ads to be successful, narrow down your audience as much as possible. So, for example, let’s say you have an accounting practice that specializes in working with small business owners, and you want to drive new clients into your business. Instead of serving a local ad to everyone in your area, you might narrow it down to business owners between the ages of 25 and 50 who live within three miles of your practice. This will ensure that the people that see your ad are people who are actually qualified to become customers.
Or let’s say you just opened up a new bakery—but you’re struggling to compete against the larger, more established bakery in town. Create a custom audience of your competitor’s Facebook followers—and then create an ad that offers them a free baked good their first time in the shop.
The point is, the more you niche down your audience—and the more targeted you create ads for those audiences—the better your results will be.
### The different types of Facebook ads
The audience you show your Facebook ad to is extremely important—but so is the type of ad you show that audience.
There are multiple different types of Face book ads and Facebook ad formats—and it’s important to understand each of them so you know which is going to be the best fit for you, your business, and your audience.
Some of the most popular (and efficient) types of advertising on Face book include:
- Link ClickAds . Link ClickAds, which send your followers to an external website (like a landing page with an email opt-in or a blog post) are arguably the most common ads on Facebook. LinkClick Ads include an image, a headline, text for the ad copy, and a link description (which gives a short summary of what people will find on the external website).
- Video Ads . Video ads are another form of LinkClick Ads. The only difference? Instead of an image, these ads feature a video (which is typically around 30 seconds long).
- Boosted Posts . If you post something on your Facebook Business page that you want to promote, you can “boost” the post with a Facebook ad to increase visibility and drive more engagement.
- Carousel ads . Advertising an ecommerce store? A Carousel ad (which allows you to feature up to 10 products, videos, or links in a single ad) is a great way to drive traffic and sales.
- Product ads . Retargeting is an extremely efficient marketing strategy—and product ads are the way to retarget your website visitors on Facebook.
- Lead ads . If your main objective is to generate leads or email opt-ins, lead ads—which allow people to sign up for your list and download your content/lead magnet straight from Facebook—should be your go-to.
- Page Like ads . Want to get more likes for your Facebook page? Page Like Ads, which include a CTA for followers to like your page, are the way to do it.
Before you move forward with running an ad on Facebook, make sure you take the time to understand the different Facebook ads—and what the best way to advertise on Facebook is for you and your goals.
### What are the Facebook advertising costs?
Ok, so clearly advertising with Facebook is a marketing strategy you want to pursue for your business. But now comes the big questions—what do Facebook ads cost?
There is, of course, an advertising on Facebook cost. But when it comes to exactly how much it costs advertising with Facebook, the answer is—it’s up to you.
When you’re in Facebook ads manager, you can set your budget for your Facebook ad campaigns. There are two types of budgets: a daily budget (which, according to Facebook, is the “average amount you’re willing to spend on an ad set or campaign per day”) and a lifetime budget (“the amount you’re willing to spend over the entire run-time of your ad set or campaign”). So, if you set a daily budget of $100, Facebook will stop showing your ads for the day once you hit that $100—then will reset the next day and start showing your ads again. If you set a lifetime budget of $1000, Facebook will stop showing your ads completely once you hit that $1000.
The beauty of this budget feature? You can advertise on Facebook with virtually any budget. Advertising with Facebook does have a minimum budget, but it’s extremely low; if your ad on Facebook gets charged by impressions, the minimum is one dollar per day. If your Face book ads get charged for clicks, likes, post engagement, or video views, the minimum is five dollars per day. And if your Facebook ad is based around what Facebook considers a “low frequency event or offer” (like an app install), the minimum daily budget is $40.
So, you set your budgets for your Facebook ads—so you’ll never spend more than you intend. And the best part? The more you optimize your ads, the further your money will go—and the more conversions you’ll be able to squeeze out of your budget.

### What’s the cost of social media advertising?
Ok, so now to the question that’s always on every marketers mind—how much is this all going to cost?
We wish we could give you a straight answer—but the truth is, the cost of social media in advertising depends on a number of factors, including which social media website you use, what kind of ads you’re leveraging, how targeted your ads are, and who you’re targeting. So, for example, a travel company looking to target millennial entrepreneurs with Instagram ads is going to need a totally different budget than a law firm prospecting new clients through Facebook ads or a marketing tool looking to build brand awareness through Snapchat video advertising.
So the bad news is, there’s no one-size-fits-all solution for how much the use of social media in advertising is going to cost your company. But the good news is there are a few surefire ways to keep costs down with advertising and social media—no matter what kind of business you’re in, who you’re targeting, and what social media platform you’re focusing on:

- Split test your ads. Split testing (also known in marketing circles as A/B testing) is a great way to squeeze the most out of every ad. All the major social media networks allow you to set both daily and lifetime budgets for your ad campaigns—and before you move forward with your campaigns, you need to double (and triple!) check those budgets. It seems like a no-brainer, but mistakes happen—and a typo or extra zero can end up costing you a ton of cash over the lifespan of your campaigns. Just to be safe, make sure to confirm your budgets (and then confirm them again!) before your ads go live.
