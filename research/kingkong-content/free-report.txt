### Starting an online business
So, you want to set up a business online—and you want to learn how to grow your business fast. So, let’s tackle first things first—the ins and outs of how to start an online business.
What’s the best online business to start?
When you’re starting an online store, the first thing you need to do is figure out what kind of business you want to start. There are so many opportunities to grow a company and setup an online business, from starting an online clothing business to launching a digital products company to offering consulting services to help your customers grow in business.
So the first question you need to answer is what’s the best online business to start?
And the answer, of course, is—it depends. You’re the person who’s going to setup an online business—so whatever business you choose needs to be one that you’re both passionate and knowledgeable about. Are you an expert on all-things-fashion? Then an ecommerce clothing store would be a great fit. Are you the go-to in your industry on how to grow a company? Then an online consulting business would be a great fit. Are you passionate about snowboarding, kitesurfing, or wakeboarding? Then an online store specializing in outdoor sports would be a slam dunk.
The kind of online business you start is up to you. But remember, you’re going to be the one driving the entire business—so when you set up an online shop, make sure it’s something you’re both passionate about and qualified to do.
Figure out your point of difference
Ecommerce is a growth business, and the online ecommerce business space is a crowded one; it seems someone is setting up an online store every day (or, more realistically, every hour). So once you’ve locked in what kind of online business you want to launch, the next step is figuring out what makes that online business different from the other 10,000 options on the market—or, in other words, your point of difference.
Think of your point of difference as your online business’ “special sauce.” It’s what makes you different, it’s what makes you unique, and it’s what’s going to help you stand out and grab your audience’s attention.
If you’re not sure what your point of difference is, it’s time to do some detective work. As yourself some deeper questions about why you’re starting an online store (or why you’re starting a new business in general) and how your business is different from your competitors. Some helpful questions for determining what sets your new business apart include:
- If I could describe my online business/brand in three words, what would they be?
- If my brand was a person, who would they be?
- What will my online business do better than anyone else in the market?
- What competitive edge do I have over other people launching online businesses?
- What’s the need I want to fill in the market? In other words, what’s missing from my industry—and how is my online business going to act as that missing link?
Getting clear on your point of difference is absolutely crucial if you want your online business to succeed. There are new businesses launching all the time, and if you don’t know what sets your business apart, it’s going to get lost in a sea of competition—and your business will never reach the level of success you deserve.
Identifying your audience
So you’ve chosen your online business model and you’ve established your point of difference. Next up when starting a new online business? Figuring out who you’re going to sell to—or, in other words, identifying your audience.
Identifying your ideal customer is an essential factor to grow your business. In order to develop a thriving, growing business, you need to serve your customers. But if you don’t know who those customers are—or what those customers want—you’re not going to get very far.
Get clear on who your online business is going to serve. What demographics—like age, occupation, location, and income level—are you going after? Where are your target demographic hanging out (so, for example, are they deeply involved in social media or do you prefer more old-school methods of communication)? What’s your ideal customers biggest problem—and how does your online business solve that problem?
Once you have a clear picture of who you’re targeting with your online business, you’ll definitely want to create an ideal customer avatar. An ideal customer avatar is an identity you create to represent your target audience—that way, when you’re doing anything for your online business (whether that’s developing a new product or rolling out a new marketing initiative), you have an actual customer to keep in mind.
So, for example, let’s say you’re launching an ecommerce shop that sells glasses and designer eyewear. Your ideal customer avatar might be Jeanette, a 35-year-old woman who wears glasses full-time and wants to have a wide variety of designer frames to choose from on a daily basis—but also wants the flexibility to be able to choose and order her frames at home. The key to creating an ideal customer avatar is to really assign your avatar an identity—it puts a face to your audience that makes building and marketing your online store much easier (and much more effective).
Once you have your ideal customer avatar, use it to lead all the decisions for your online business. When you’re developing a product, think of how your ideal customer avatar would react to it. If you’re on the fence about a marketing or advertising strategy, keep your ideal customer avatar—and how they’d react to the strategy—front of mind. Your building your online business in order to connect with your customers—and your ideal customer avatar is a great way to make sure everything you do in your business works to foster that connection.

### The basics of paid search and pay per click advertising
Before we jump into how to generate real results with cost per click Adwords, let’s take a minute to explain PPC and the basics of PPC internet marketing.
So, first things first—what’s a PPC? PPC stands for “pay per click,” and it’s an advertising model where—you guessed it—you pay for every click on your ad. There are two sides to the Google search paid model: Google Display Ads and Google Search Network. With both models, you can create a PPC campaign that targets your ideal demographic. But the way those PPC ads are shown is totally different.
Let’s start with Google Search Network. Google Search Network is the text-based ads that are shown on the top of the search engine results pages (SERPs) whenever someone searches for a keyword. So, for example, let’s say you own an interior design firm in Sydney and you want to generate new business through the Google Search Network. You would create your PPC ad and then use PPC bidding to target keywords that your ideal customer may be searching for (like “Sydney interior designer” or “Home renovation Sydney”). Then, when your potential customers search for those keywords, your ad would pop up—and if they click on it, you’d pay Google for driving that lead to your business (the cost per click will depend on how well your ad converts and how competitive your keywords are).
Google Display Network works a bit differently. With Google Display Network, you can place display ads (also known as banner ads) on websites you think your ideal customers might frequent. So, using the interior design example, you might place ads on websites that popular interior design blogs or ecommerce shops that sell furniture and home accessories.
Google Display Network and Google Search Network are both effective PPC marketing platforms; the best fit is going to depend on your business, your product or service, and your goals. So, for example, if you want to get your messaging in front of potential customers when they’re ready to make a purchase, using a paid Google search and targeting purchase-related keywords is a no-brainer. If you want to boost your website conversion rate, a retargeting PPC campaign on Google Display Network can drive serious results. Try campaigns on Google’s search and display networks and figure out which PPC marketing strategy is the best fit for you and your goals.

### What makes the best PPC campaigns stand out?
PPC is an incredibly effective marketing strategy—but not all PPC campaigns are created equal.
When people search on Google, they’re immediately exposed to an endless amount of information. If you want your PPC ads to succeed, you need to break through that clutter, grab their attention, and show them why whatever you have to offer is better than what they’re going to find as they scroll through the SERPs.
But how, exactly, do you do that? What are the secrets to making your ads PPC stand out?
- Use persuasive language. If you’re creating a text-based PPC ad for Google Search Network, your copy is all you have to make your ad stand out—so that copy needs to be persuasive. The right language is key to driving clicks on your ad—and driving results for your PPC campaigns. Use persuasive, emotional language that plays to your ideal audience’s needs and wants. So, for example, let’s say you’re creating a PPC ad to advertise your new line of high-end cookware. Instead of an ad that reads “Buy cookware here,” go for something that plays to your audience’s emotional needs, like “Tired of wasting hours waiting for your dinner to cook? These pots and pans will have dinner cooked and on the table in 30 minutes or less!” By using persuasive language that plays to your customers’ needs and struggles (in this case, the struggle with spending too much time on dinner—and the need to have the process go faster), you immediately grab their attention—and increase the likelihood they’ll click on your ad.
- Use an attention-grabbing headline. Speaking of copy, the most important copy in your ad? Your headline. The headline is what’s going to initially draw people’s attention—so make sure it’s bold, catchy, and clearly sets the stage for the rest of the ad.
- Make sure your banner ads are well-designed. If you’re focused your PPC campaign on Google Display Network, make sure you invest the time and resources into creating well-designed banner ads. Your banner ads need to jump off the screen and grab people’s attention, so make sure to use key design elements (like typography, hierarchy, and layout) and eye-catching visuals to design an ad that drives results.
If you follow these paid search best practices, your ads are sure to break through the clutter and connect with your ideal audience (and drive crazy conversions in the process).
### Tips to bring down your average cost per click with Google PPC
So, clearly, if you want to succeed in today’s marketing landscape, you need to be focused on cost per click advertising and PPC management. But the question on everyone’s mind, of course, is—how much does a click cost?
And the answer is—it depends. There’s no one-size-fits-all budget for pay per click advertising. Your AdWords cost per click is going to vary based on a host of factors, including who you’re targeting, how competitive your keywords are, and how well your ads are optimised.
Obviously, you want to keep your PPC charges to a minimum. But how, exactly, do you do that? How do you lower your AdWords cost per click and make the most of your PPC budget?
- Split test your ads. The best thing you can do to lower the amount you have to shell out for PPC payment? Split test your ads. Split testing (also known as A/B testing) is a way to continually optimise your ads and increase performance—and lower costs as a result. Create an ad, and then create a duplicate ad that changes a single element (like the headline or CTA). Then, run the two ads against each other. See which performs better, and then repeat the process with a different element. Keep split testing your ads until you have a PPC ad that’s converting like crazy.
- Start with a small budget—and work your way up. The last thing you want to do is blow your entire PPC budget before you’ve had a chance to figure out what’s working—and optimise your ads accordingly. Keep budgets low as you experiment with different ad formats, copy, and split testing. Once you get your PPC campaign to a point where it’s converting at a high rate, then you can roll out a bigger budget. Starting with a smaller budget—and increasing your budget once your ads are performing—is a great way to stretch your resources (and keep your cost per click low).
- Keep an eye on your PPC statistics. If you want to make the most of your PPC budget, you need to have a firm understanding of how each ad and each campaign is performing. Stay on top of your PPC statistics and analytics. Know where your budget is going, what keywords are performing, and how much you’re spending per click. The more you know about your campaigns, the more you can optimise them—and the more you’ll be able to stretch your budget.
