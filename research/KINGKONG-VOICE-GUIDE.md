# King Kong® — Brand Voice, Tone & Language Analysis

> Source: full crawl of https://kingkong.co (Sept 2026) — 95 pages, with close reading of all
> seven service pages: `/agency/`, `/seo-agency/`, `/ppc-management-agency/`,
> `/facebook-advertising-agency/`, `/conversion-rate-optimization-agency/`,
> `/landing-page-agency/`, `/web-design-agency/`, plus homepage and About.
> Purpose: a reusable voice specification for writing OMD landing page copy in this register.

---

## 0. First, the thing you should know

**OMD's current homepage is already a direct adaptation of King Kong's homepage.** Not "similar in
spirit" — substantially the same copy with the nouns swapped. Compare:

| King Kong (`/`) | OMD (`/`) |
|---|---|
| "Dear business builder," | "Dear Clinic Builder," |
| "Growing a business is hard. Really f*cking hard." | "Growing a clinic is hard – really hard." |
| "Stressed and sleeping like a teething two-year-old." | "Stressed, restless, and losing sleep like a teething two-year-old." |
| "As your hair goes fifty shades of grey from all the uncertainty." | "Watching your hair turn fifty shades of grey from all the uncertainty." |
| "You're being pulled in a bazillion different directions." | "You're being pulled in a thousand different directions." |
| "Ohmygawd! Should I blog? Start a podcast? Build a personal brand? Document my whole flippin' life on Instagram?" | "Ohmygawd! Should I blog? Be on TikTok? Build a personal brand? Document my entire life on Instagram?" |
| "More security. More money. More freedom. More memories. More time with your family. More business-class flights with your spouse to places that result in more sand between your toes." | *(identical, plus "More respect from your colleagues")* |
| "Hire us to do it for you" / "Learn how to do it yourself" | "Hire Us To Do it for you" / "Learn How To Do it Yourself" |
| "We call this shit show a rats nest. Also known as hell." | "We call this the fog of uncertainty." |

So this isn't a new direction — it's **turning up a dial OMD is already on**. The useful question
is which King Kong devices OMD has *not* yet adopted, and which ones it should never adopt because
a medical audience is regulated. Sections 6 and 7 cover both.

## 1. The core voice in one paragraph

King Kong writes **classic long-form direct response** — Gary Halbert / Dan Kennedy sales-letter
structure — delivered in the voice of **a brash, funny, slightly unhinged mate who has made a lot
of money and is impatient with bullshit.** It is aggressively conversational, profane, relentlessly
second-person, and built almost entirely from **one-line paragraphs**. It alternates between
insulting the competition, flattering the reader's ambition, and dropping enormous specific numbers
as proof. Nothing is ever hedged. Every claim is either a number or a boast.

The governing principle, stated on their own SEO page: *"It might make us come across as brash, but
that's only because the brutal truth is so rarely spoken these days."*

## 2. The page architecture (every service page follows this exact skeleton)

1. **"BREAKING NEWS" ticker bar** — scrolling, red, urgent. e.g. *"Australia's #1 Fastest Growing Ad Agency Reveals…"*
2. **Headline as a news bulletin or "weird trick" promise**, usually in quotes:
   *"Finally! A Guaranteed, But 'Weird' New Way To Rank #1 On Google In 90 Days or Less"*
3. **A dateline** — *"Updated: 9th of September, 2026"* — so the letter feels written today
4. **"Dear Business Builder,"** — the salutation, every single time
5. **The hook formula**: *"If you would like to [outcome]… Then this will be the most exciting message you'll ever read. Here's why:"*
6. **Agitate the pain** — the reader's ads shit the bed, their bank account screams, they throw their MacBook out the window
7. **Name the villain** — "filthy, lying SEO agency scumbags", "shady rug merchants", "almond latte drinking marketing experts"
8. **Establish authority with enormous specific numbers** — $10.2 billion, 1,184 industries, $200m ad spend
9. **Teach something real** — 3 genuine, useful steps (SKAGs, don't-make-ads-look-like-ads, HVCO)
10. **Cut the lesson off mid-stream** — *"But look, this page is getting long… these are just 3 of 12 steps. Wanna see all 12?"*
11. **The lead magnet** — free report, with a bulleted list of curiosity-gap benefits
12. **The guarantee** — beat your results by 30% or we work for free
13. **Qualification / takeaway close** — "this is not for everybody", three conditions you must meet
14. **Scarcity** — "only taking 8 new clients per month", "spots almost gone for September"
15. **The P.S.** — always long, always the hardest-hitting emotional passage on the page
16. **"TL;DR Version"** — a short recap for skimmers
17. **FAQ block** — noticeably calmer, more informative, SEO-oriented
18. **CTA button**: *"Hit The Damn Button"*

**Note the tonal split:** the sales letter is wild; the FAQ at the bottom is sane, helpful and
almost normal. That's deliberate — the FAQ does the SEO and objection-handling work, so the letter
above it is free to be pure entertainment.

## 3. Sentence-level mechanics

**One thought per line. Constant paragraph breaks.**
> You see, your ad has one purpose, and that is to get people to CLICK!
>
> Your ads must DEMAND attention.
>
> Don't be boring, boring doesn't stand out and demand attention!
>
> Boring doesn't get clicks.
>
> Boring murders your ROI.

They say so explicitly on the Facebook page: *"Keep it simple and use short sentences. One thought
per sentence, so you're easily understood. Don't use words that make people think."*

**Other devices, all heavily used:**

| Device | Example |
|---|---|
| **Direct address / commands** | "Come closer. Listen." · "Alright, saddle up." · "Walk with me…" · "Hold up, bucko." |
| **Stage directions in asterisks** | `*wink*` · `*rolls eyes*` · `*grabs popcorn*` · `*cue angels singing*` · `*yawn*` · `*clears throat*` |
| **Self-interruption** | "But look, this page is getting long…" · "Anyhoo." · "But I'll stop you right there." |
| **Rhetorical question → blunt answer** | "Are Facebook Ads really effective? **Is water really wet?**" |
| **Fake-out** | "There's a solution to all this madness. Here it is: there is no solution. Calm down, jeez! I'm just kiddin'." |
| **Sound effects / interjections** | "Ohmygawd!" · "*gasp*" · "BOOM!" · "Errrrrrrbody's talkin' bout chatbots." |
| **Deliberate misspelling for speech rhythm** | "lemme" · "gotta" · "imma" · "yea" · "'em" · "waaaaaay" · "truuuuust me" |
| **ALL CAPS for one word** | "This is the quickest and most certain way to **MURDER** your money!" |
| **Parenthetical asides** | "(not one in a hundred agencies even have a clue this is possible)" |
| **Absurd specificity** | "$193 PER CLICK" · "1,351,065,352 impressions" · "1,184 industries" |
| **Nicknames for the reader** | "bucko" · "chicken nugget" · "tiger" · "bizzsnaps" · "you greedy, little truth-seeker" |

## 4. The metaphor system

King Kong's imagery is drawn from a consistent well: **violence, war, hunting, money, appetite,
and bodily discomfort.** This is what gives it its texture.

**Violence / war:** "battle-tested" · "battle-hardened" · "stone-cold killer" · "a pack of media
buying and copywriting savages" · "crushed into a fine powder that scatters in the wind" · "your
website gets zipped up in a body bag and taken to the morgue" · "we send them out to war every day…
we want them to take prisoners" · "murder it with a chainsaw" · "grab your prospects by the throat"
· "apex predator" · "put their neck in a noose"

**Appetite / hunger:** "itchy-to-buy prospects" · "spend like sailors on leave" · "starving crowds"
· "wallet out ready-to-buy" · "begging to buy" · "hunt mode" · "greed glands"

**Flood / volume:** "a torrential downpour of customers" · "open the floodgates" · "suck in sales
like a vacuum cleaner on steroids" · "your pipeline got completely flooded overnight"

**Absurd physical discomfort:** "we'd rather rub scorpion chillies in our eyes" · "eating a mouthful
of fire ants while riding a motorbike blindfolded through the snow at 100km per hour, naked" ·
"walking across a live minefield stark naked and blindfolded" · "somebody please pull out my eyelashes"

**Money made physical:** "turn your website into a $$$ printer" · "money-printing machine" · "you
can't take rankings to the bank" · "lemme just withdraw all of my money in crisp $100 bills and
throw it at Google" · "your bank account is left screaming for mercy"

**Sex / seduction (for funnels):** "selling is like seduction, and seduction works best if there's a
little foreplay" · "a bit like walking up to a complete stranger in the street and asking them to
marry you" · "a sexy well-dressed super-powered salesperson"

## 5. The recurring arguments (transferable to any service)

These are the *ideas* underneath the jokes. They're genuinely good and they port directly.

1. **"Marketers lie, numbers don't."** Refuse to argue; produce a figure.
2. **"You can't take rankings to the bank."** Attack vanity metrics. Rankings, impressions, clicks,
   traffic — worthless unless they become revenue. *"Look honey, we rank #1 on Google!" *wipes tear**
3. **"Most people don't have a traffic problem. They have a conversion problem."** The entire
   landing-page and CRO thesis in one line.
4. **"It's not about HOW you ask for the sale, it's about WHEN."** Justifies lead magnets and funnels.
5. **"Don't make your ads look like ads."** Make them look like the content people already consume.
6. **"We have skin in the game."** The guarantee reframes risk as shared.
7. **"One size does not fit all."** Used to refuse published pricing and attack anyone with a price list.
8. **"We take our own medicine."** Most agencies don't use the services they sell. (Note the
   meta-move: the free-report offer says *"…and BOOM! That's how it's done… we take our own medicine."*)
9. **"Guesswork is the enemy."** Named villain: hoping-and-praying, shiny object syndrome, the rats nest.
10. **"When you pray for the rain, you have to deal with the mud."** Qualification — you'd better be
    able to handle the volume.

## 6. What OMD has NOT yet borrowed (the available upside)

OMD took the empathy letter and the two-path CTA. It left these on the table:

- **The dateline** (`Updated: 11th of September, 2026`) — makes a page feel live, costs nothing
- **The "BREAKING NEWS" ticker** above the hero
- **The "most exciting message you'll ever read" hook formula**
- **The cut-off teach** — give 3 of 7 steps, gate the rest behind a lead magnet. OMD gives away all
  7 steps of the Patient Generation System for free on the page. That's a conversion leak.
- **The named villain.** OMD is vague about who failed the reader. King Kong is vivid and specific.
  OMD's case studies *do* contain the raw material — "a local digital marketing agency… couldn't
  observe a significant increase", "one even got his website penalised by Google" — it's just never
  dramatised.
- **The long P.S.** — King Kong's highest-intensity copy lives here
- **The "TL;DR Version"** for skimmers
- **The qualification/takeaway close** — "there are three things that must be true before you apply"
- **A calm, genuinely useful FAQ** sitting under a wild letter (OMD's FAQ is good; the letter above
  it is tamer than it could be)
- **Review-count social proof in the CTA** — "4.6 stars out of 607 reviews" beside every button

## 7. What must NOT be carried across

This matters, and it's not a stylistic preference — it's regulatory. OMD sells to AHPRA-, TGA-,
GMC-, ASA- and HIPAA-regulated clinicians, and OMD's own SEO page already states: *"No honest
digital marketing agency can guarantee specific search rankings."*

| King Kong device | Why it can't transfer |
|---|---|
| **Profanity** — "really f*cking hard", "shit the bed", "arse" | Wrong register for consultant surgeons; OMD already softened this to "really hard" |
| **"We work for free" / "rank #1 in 90 days guaranteed"** | Contradicts OMD's own published position that ranking guarantees are dishonest. Don't reintroduce it. |
| **Naming competitors as criminals** — "filthy, lying scumbags", "shady rug merchants" | Defamation exposure, and it reads as unprofessional to a medical buyer. Attack *practices*, not *people*. |
| **Sexual metaphors** — "seduction", "foreplay", "honeymoon hotel room" | Inappropriate beside cosmetic-surgery and women's-health clients |
| **Violence imagery at full strength** — "body bag", "morgue", "murder", "noose" | Tonally impossible next to medical services |
| **Unsubstantiated round numbers** | OMD's numbers must stay tied to real, named, verifiable case studies — AHPRA rules on testimonials and misleading claims apply to OMD's clients and by extension its collateral |
| **"Only 8 clients per month" as pure urgency theatre** | OMD's "2 new clients per month" is fine *because it's justified* ("due to the work involved onboarding"). Keep the justification. |

## 8. The calibrated register for OMD — "King Kong at 70%"

The target is: **same structure, same rhythm, same specificity, same refusal to be boring — but the
swagger is earned through evidence rather than aggression, and the humour is dry rather than crude.**

**Keep:** one-thought-per-line rhythm · "Dear Clinic Builder" letter framing · dateline · direct
address · the occasional `*wink*` · rhetorical question → blunt answer · vanity-metric attacks ·
huge specific numbers · the cut-off teach · the long P.S. · the TL;DR · qualification close ·
justified scarcity · calm FAQ under a punchy letter.

**Swap:**

| King Kong | OMD equivalent |
|---|---|
| "Really f*cking hard" | "Really hard." (already done — keep it) |
| "Filthy, lying SEO agency scumbags" | "The agency that sold you a website you can't edit and a report you can't read." |
| "You can't take rankings to the bank" | **"You can't put impressions on your surgery list."** ← this is the money line for OMD |
| "Itchy-to-buy prospects who spend like sailors on leave" | "Patients who've already decided — they're just choosing between you and the clinic down the road." |
| "A torrential downpour of customers" | "A full consultation diary." |
| "Hit The Damn Button" | "Let's Talk" / "Show Me How" — keep the imperative energy, drop the aggression |
| "Apex predator in your market" | "The obvious choice in your city." |
| "Money-printing machine" | "A website that books consultations while you're in theatre." |

**The single most important adaptation:** King Kong's reader wants *money*. OMD's reader wants money
too — but is embarrassed to say so, because they're a clinician. OMD's existing copy already
navigates this beautifully with the ladder: *"More security. More money. More respect from your
colleagues. More freedom. More time with your family."* **"More respect from your colleagues"** is
OMD's own addition to King Kong's list and it is the sharpest line in their copy — it names the
status anxiety that actually drives a surgeon. Build on that, not on greed glands.

**And the ready-made villain:** King Kong attacks other agencies. OMD's more powerful and more
truthful antagonist is already written on their site — *the less-skilled competitor who markets
better.* That's Huyen's founding story, that's the Van Gogh parable, and it is the exact fear a
good surgeon feels. Use it.

## 9. Worked example — the same offer in three registers

**Generic agency (what most medical marketers write):**
> Our comprehensive SEO solutions are designed to improve your clinic's online visibility and drive
> qualified traffic to your website, helping you attract more patients and grow your practice.

**King Kong (unusable as-is for medical):**
> Listen up, bucko. Most SEO agencies have it all wrong. They focus on rankings, pixie glitter, and
> not revenue. Because you can't take rankings to the bank. You can't use them to pay your
> employees' salaries. And you sure as hell can't use them to buy yourself a shiny new Range Rover
> Sport. *wipes tear*

**OMD at the right calibration:**
> Most medical marketing agencies will show you a chart going up and to the right.
>
> Rankings. Impressions. "Reach."
>
> Here's the problem.
>
> You can't put impressions on your surgery list.
>
> You can't pay your practice manager with reach.
>
> And no patient in the history of medicine has ever booked a deep plane facelift because a clinic
> ranked #4 for a keyword nobody searches.
>
> So let's talk about the only two numbers that matter: how many people enquired, and how many of
> them ended up on your table.
>
> That's what we track. From the first click to the booked procedure. *(Ask your current agency if
> they can do that. We'll wait.)*

## 10. Quick-reference checklist for writing OMD copy in this voice

- [ ] Does it open with a dated, personal letter to one named reader ("Dear Clinic Builder")?
- [ ] Is the longest paragraph under three lines?
- [ ] Is there a specific, verifiable number in the first 100 words?
- [ ] Have I named the enemy — vaguely competent competitors, vanity metrics, the last agency?
- [ ] Have I taught something genuinely useful before asking for anything?
- [ ] Have I cut the teach off and gated the rest?
- [ ] Is there at least one line that would make a surgeon actually laugh?
- [ ] Does every claim attach to a named clinic and a real figure?
- [ ] Is there a long P.S. that lands the emotional blow?
- [ ] Is there a TL;DR for the skimmer?
- [ ] Does the close *qualify* the reader rather than beg them?
- [ ] Is the scarcity justified, not asserted?
- [ ] Have I stripped every guarantee that AHPRA or the GMC would not permit?
- [ ] Is the FAQ calm, useful and free of swagger?
